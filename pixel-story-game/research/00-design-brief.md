# 00 — Design brief distilled from research notes 01–06

This brief condenses the six research notes into the constraints every
concept for the game must satisfy. It is the input for concept
generation; the full evidence and sources are in notes 01–06.

## 1. Goal and business frame

- **Product:** a completely original, premium pixel-art story game built
  for maximum virality at release and maximum lifetime profit.
- **Studio frame:** 3–5 people, 2.5–3 years, $0.6–1.0M all-in. The
  conservative case (~30k lifetime units) must be survivable; the base
  case (~160k units) returns 1.5–3× budget; breakout (1M+) is the target.
- **Price:** $19.99 base, 15% launch discount, Deluxe $29.99 (OST,
  artbook, commentary). A free 60–90 minute prologue/demo is the funnel
  and stays up permanently. (Virality data favours $10–15; a concept may
  argue for $14.99 if its length is shorter.)
- **Platforms:** Steam + Steam Deck Verified at launch; Switch/Switch 2
  at launch if a partner funds it, otherwise +3–6 months; PS5 +3–6
  months; Xbox +12 months; premium mobile +18–24 months.
- **Languages at launch:** English, Simplified Chinese, Japanese,
  Korean. Brazilian Portuguese and LATAM Spanish at +3 months. Humor
  must survive transcreation; puns cannot carry the comedy.
- **Never:** Early Access for a linear story, IP assignment to a
  publisher, generative-AI assets of any kind, crypto/NFT/token
  mechanics (banned on Steam), gambling/loot boxes/real-money drops,
  botted metrics.

## 2. Length, structure, and the 2-hour refund window

- Main path **6–9 hours**, delivered as **chapters of 60–120 minutes**
  so each fits one stream session and ends on a cliffhanger.
- Replay, branch, and secret layers push completionist time to 15+ hours.
- **No complete arc resolves before hour ~5** (refund safety), but a
  "this game is not what you think" turn lands between minute 60 and 120.
- Opening: a clip-worthy beat in the **first 5 minutes**, the premise
  jolt by minute 10, a character players love by minute 30, then a clip
  beat every 8–12 minutes, each readable by a cold viewer in under 2 s.

## 3. Virality engines (a concept must use at least six, concretely)

- **E1 Reaction-moment density:** storyboarded clip beats.
- **E2 Divergence and comparison:** named endings or states, a shareable
  result card with "X% of players", 1–3 ultra-rare outcomes.
- **E3 The game remembers you:** persistent memory across saves,
  resets, or chapters that changes *mechanics*, not just dialogue.
- **E4 Three-tier secrets:** everyone / completionist / community-only,
  ideally one puzzle where each player holds a unique fragment.
- **E5 Lore-theory bait:** an unpublished canonical timeline, deliberate
  gaps, recurring symbols, partial confirmations over time.
- **E6 Meme-able character + remixable leitmotif:** iconic silhouette,
  catchphrase, signature sound; OST cleared for creators day one.
- **E7 Social layer:** for a single-player story game, an asynchronous
  "echo" layer (other players' choices or traces appear in your world)
  or a small 2-player side mode — not a friendslop clone.
- **E8 Escalating spectacle** (optional for story games).
- **E9 Premise shock:** the pitch lands in **under 8 words** and contains
  a self-contradicting image (cute/lethal, mundane/absurd).
- **E10 Ambient presence** (optional; botting backlash risk).
- **E11 Events and free funnels:** free prologue, dated releases or
  in-game events everyone experiences together.

Also required: a **streamer mode** (DMCA-safe music, spoiler-safe end
cards, chat-vote hooks or viewer names in the game), **per-save
randomised or seeded solutions** so a VOD never fully spoils a run, and
a **native 9:16 capture mode**.

## 4. Narrative and fandom rules

- A cast of **distinct individuals, never mobs**. Every named character
  has a silhouette readable at 16–32 px, one signature colour, one
  cosplay-able prop, a want, and a **verbal tic expressed through text
  formatting** (case, brackets, punctuation).
- Sincere core under the comedy; comedy must be situational/visual so it
  travels internationally.
- Each lead keeps one deliberate gap (ambiguity breeds fan work).
- **Two versions of each character** are a proven fandom engine, but the
  "dream world vs. real world" split is taken (OMORI) — find a new axis.
- **The core verb carries the theme** and the theme is pitchable in one
  sentence.
- Emotional curve: comfort/comedy → attachment through small acts of
  care → rupture → catharsis the *player performs* → lingering question.
  Plant a concrete motif early, pay it off with a musical reprise plus a
  player action.
- Answer the emotional questions fully; leave one cosmological question
  open; seed one rarely-seen unexplained figure.
- Darkness on the box: advertise the premise honestly; surprise players
  with *how*, not *that*.
- Themes that travel: friendship, found family, grief, forgiveness,
  parent–child reconciliation, belonging. Avoid country-specific satire,
  religious iconography, gore/skeleton-heavy imagery (licensed-China
  friction), and self-harm as spectacle.

## 5. Originality guard — markers that earn an "X clone" label

Do not build around any of these:

- *Undertale-like:* a guide/flower that knows your save; "I know you
  reset"; a soul dodging bullets in a box; Spare/Mercy as the morality
  system; Pacifist/Genocide routes; skeleton puns; EarthBound suburban
  weirdness as the whole identity.
- *OMORI-like:* a headspace/dream world hiding childhood trauma; the
  friend group "before the incident"; "the protagonist caused the death."
- *Yume Nikki / RPG Maker horror-like:* silent girl wandering dreams,
  collectible effects, chase sequences.
- *Stardew-like:* inherited farm, failing town, evil corporate store,
  stamina bar, marriage-candidate roster.
- *Celeste-like:* precision platformer as mental-health allegory.
- *Coffee Talk-like:* bartender/barista serving fantasy patrons.
- *Generic:* "it was all a dream/coma", telegraphed cute-world-dark-secret,
  fake save deletion or fake crash with no mechanical consequence,
  "you were the killer all along", content warnings used as marketing.

## 6. Market red zone

Generic pixel metroidvania, survivors-likes, farming/life sims, pure
visual novels, PS1/VHS/analog horror walkers, Exit-8 anomaly clones,
friendslop clones, twist-less roguelike deckbuilders, a time loop as the
headline hook, adult/edgy content as the hook, anything live-service or
gacha.

## 7. White space (demand > quality supply)

- **W1** Pixel deduction mystery with a real emotional story, 4–8 h.
- **W2** 2D narrative job sim with dread or anomalies (3D first-person
  versions are selling: Shift At Midnight, Retro Rewind, Dispatch).
- **W3** Narrative roguelite where each run reveals the mystery.
- **W4** Short pixel psychological horror-RPG, handheld-first, CN/JP
  localised (Look Outside: solo dev, 500k units).
- **W5** Story-driven desktop companion / idle game.
- **W6** Asymmetric 2–4 player co-op mystery with split clues.
- **W7** Human-made games *about* AI, automation, or job anxiety,
  marketed as human-made (85% of players negative on gen-AI).
- **W8** Anime-styled pixel story about internet culture, CN/JP/KR at
  launch (Needy Streamer Overload lineage).

## 8. Presentation rules

- Single-frame signature: one palette, one silhouette, one UI frame; a
  screenshot shrunk to 184×69 must still read as *this* game.
- Native 320×180 or 640×360 (integer-scales to 720p/1080p/1440p/4K);
  expand the view for 16:10 Deck rather than letterbox.
- Keep action, faces and dialogue inside a centre 9:16 safe zone; ship a
  vertical capture mode.
- Animation money goes to talking faces first: portraits, blinks, text
  effects, per-character voice sounds.
- Illustrated (non-pixel or ultra-chunky) Steam capsule by a specialist;
  logo legible at 120×45.
- Text legible on a 7–8" handheld; readable-font toggle; text speed,
  backlog, skip-seen; shake/flash toggles; assist options.
- Music: one hummable leitmotif arranged at least three ways; hybrid
  chip + live instruments; OST on Steam/Bandcamp/streaming day one.
