## 1. Character design for fandom

**1.1 Every character is an individual, including the "enemies."** Toby Fox has said it was important that every monster feel like an individual, unlike interchangeable RPG mobs ([Wikipedia: Sans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sans_(Undertale)), [List of characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Undertale_and_Deltarune_characters)).
- Throwaway encounters such as Temmie, Napstablook and Muffet became fan-art staples [bg].
- Each named NPC is a free roll of the dice for fan attention.

**1.2 Silhouette, signature color and one prop.** The breakout characters read clearly at 16 to 32 px and in a thumbnail [bg analysis]:
- Sans: round skull, blue hoodie, glowing eye.
- Papyrus: tall, with a red scarf.
- The Knight: a white horned mask on a black body.
- Hornet: red cloak and needle.
- Spamton: pink and yellow with salesman shades.
- OMORI: a monochrome boy among saturated friends. Here the *absence* of color is the signature.

A fan artist should be able to draw the character from memory in under a minute. A cosplayer should be able to build it from one hoodie, mask or cloak.

**1.3 A verbal tic shown through text formatting.** These are quotable, screenshot-able and meme-able [bg]:
- Sans speaks in lowercase puns.
- Papyrus speaks in ALL CAPS with "NYEH HEH HEH."
- Spamton uses bracketed "[[BIG SHOT]]" hyperlink-speak.
- Temmie says "hOI!"

Fox has said the art is deliberately simple because "visual gags usually benefit from funny, expressive drawings" ([Killscreen](https://www.killscreen.com/behind-humor-toby-foxs-undertale/)).

**1.4 Ambiguity invites interpretation and shipping.** Fox describes Sans as "a reliable, friendly, humorous guy… you never quite know what his deal is" ([OFF interview](https://offtherpg.com/interviews/tobyfox/)).
- That gap is where Undertale's largest pairing, Sans/Reader, lives ([Fanlore](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Undertale)).
- The 145 pre-release Lace fics show the same thing: fans will write about a silhouette with a few lines of dialogue.
- Ambiguous does not mean blank. Give the character a strong surface and an unexplained interior.

**1.5 Two versions of each character give fans built-in alternate universes.** Fanlore notes that OMORI's fandom "mostly revolved around its characters, especially their dream world counterparts" ([Fanlore: OMORI](https://fanlore.org/wiki/OMORI)).
- Headspace versus real world, Light World versus Dark World, and Undertale's fan-made "AUs" (Underswap, Underfell) all show that "what if this character were different" is a content engine [bg].

**1.6 Interiority drawn from the creator's own life.**
- OMOCAT built Sunny as a silent protagonist "whose silence is a part of their personality, but who thinks a lot," based on their own high-school years ([OMORI wiki: Sunny](https://omori.wiki/Sunny)).
- OMOCAT calls Basil the most complicated character, with hobbies taken from OMOCAT's own life ([OMORI wiki: Basil](https://omori.wiki/Basil)).
- Specific, lived-in details produce fan analysis. Generic ones produce indifference.

**1.7 Shippability is structural.** Small ensembles with charged pairs (rivals, caretaker and ward, two people who share a secret) generate ships whether romance is a mechanic or not. Examples [bg]:
- Romance as a mechanic: Hades' Zagreus/Thanatos/Megaera, Stardew's 12 marriage candidates.
- No romance mechanic: OMORI's friend group.

**1.8 Merch fit.** Plush-able blob shapes (Temmie, the Knight) and strong two-color schemes carry over directly to Fangamer-style merch lines, which Undertale, Deltarune, OMORI and Hollow Knight all sustain [bg].

## 2. Emotional architecture

**The recurring hit curve:** comfort and comedy → attachment through small acts of care → rupture (betrayal, reveal or loss) → catharsis the *player performs* → a lingering open question.

- **Undertale [bg]:**
  - Flowey's "kill or be killed" betrayal arrives within about five minutes.
  - Toriel's pie and protection come next, and the game's thesis is tested by the Toriel fight inside the first hour.
  - Papyrus's "date" is pure comedy before the True Lab horror.
  - In the finale, the player's own SAVE command becomes the emotional act, while "Hopes and Dreams" reprises the opening motif.
- **OMORI [bg]:** a bright Headspace comedy of friendship hides a real-world estrangement. The reveal is paid off musically by "Final Duet" (Sunny's violin and Mari's piano) and resolved by a player choice about forgiveness.
- **To the Moon:**
  - Kan Gao wrote cues tied to timed scene moments ([GameSpot postmortem](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/to-the-moon-postmortem-qanda-with-kan-gao/1100-6347050/), [Super Jump](https://medium.com/super-jump/freebird-games-more-than-storytelling-69eece87a538)).
  - He relies on restraint, letting dialogue "fill in the gaps" ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/69637/indie-to-moon-explores-memory-and-regret)).
  - Conversations about the game open with "did you cry?" ([Killscreen](https://www.killscreen.com/bird-story/)). The tears are part of how it spreads.
- **Celeste [bg]:** the "depression monster" Badeline is reconciled with rather than defeated, and the reconciliation becomes a *mechanic* (the Badeline boost). Gameplay and theme resolve together.
- **Hades [bg]:** catharsis is spread across dozens of runs. Each death unlocks new dialogue, so failure feeds the story.

**How "I cried at a pixel game" gets made:**
1. Plant a concrete motif early: paper rabbits and a platypus, a violin and piano, a pie.
2. Let it gather meaning through repetition.
3. Pay it off with a **musical reprise plus a player action**.

Low-resolution abstraction helps: players project their own faces onto a 12-pixel sprite.

**Short games (2 to 10 hours) still create obsession through:**
- Replay structures: multiple endings, cross-save memory.
- Unresolved "mystery residue."
- A twist that must not be spoiled, which turns every fan into a recruiter ("just play it blind").

Examples include Doki Doki Literature Club (DDLC), Mouthwashing, Slay the Princess and To the Moon, which run about 2 to 5 hours per pass [bg].

## 3. Choice and consequence that drives "what did you do?"

- **The game remembers [bg].**
  - Undertale's Flowey comments on reloads.
  - A Genocide run permanently taints later Pacifist endings. This is persistent memory outside the save file.
  - DDLC manipulates its own character files.
  - Deltarune tells players up front that "your choices don't matter," then hides the "Weird Route" (Snowgrave), which needs deliberate, sustained cruelty. That contradiction alone produced endless discussion.
- **Outcomes that let players compare runs.** In Slay the Princess:
  - The Princess's voice and form are "mapped roughly to the optional dialogues you picked."
  - There are about **20 million permutations** of root progressions.
  - The base game has 6,000 lines, and the Pristine Cut adds 2,500 ([Comics Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/exclusive-slay-the-princess-the-pristine-cut-black-tabby-games/)).
  - The team pruned routes so that each form "felt like it represented players" ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/pax-slay-the-princess-interview-demo-impressions/), [Game Developer deep-dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/deep-dive-player-centered-narrative-design-in-slay-the-princess)).
  - "Which Princess did you get?" is the shareable object.
- **Global choice statistics [bg].** Telltale's "X% of players chose…" screens and Detroit: Become Human's flowcharts with world percentages turn a single-player choice into a social comparison.
- **Morally weighted mechanics [bg].**
  - Papers, Please has 20 endings, and its family-starvation pressure makes paperwork a moral act.
  - Return of the Obra Dinn confirms fates only in sets of three. That stops guessing and creates progress players can report ("I'm at 42/60") ([obradinn.com](https://obradinn.com/)).
- **Achievement and true-ending rates could not be fetched this session.** Undertale and Deltarune ship with **no Steam achievements** [bg], so hidden routes are found socially through wikis, Reddit and video, which itself drives community traffic. ## 4. Mystery and lore depth

- **W.D. Gaster [bg].**
  - Gaster exists only through rare events gated by a hidden random "fun value," Wingdings text, and "Entry Number 17."
  - Only some players ever see the evidence, so it spreads through screenshots and data-mining.
  - Toby Fox has left him unexplained for more than ten years. Deltarune's opening voice ("the Goner maker") keeps the thread alive.
- **Hollow Knight.** Lore lives in item text and environment, and many figures are never explained. The seven-year Silksong wait was itself turned into content:
  - the "clown makeup" meme ([KYM](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/hollow-knight-silksong-fans-in-clown-makeup))
  - a fan's daily drawing streak, which reached 320 drawings ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/hollow-knight-silksong/fan-art))
  - "silkposting" through launch day ([Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/even-the-hollow-knight-silksong-release-day-couldnt-slow-down-rsi/z68554))

  At launch in September 2025, Silksong peaked at about 587k concurrent players on Steam [bg].
- **Inscryption.** In his GDC 2022 post-mortem ([slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2022/Speaker+Slides/GDC22_Inscryption_Post-Mortem.pdf)), Daniel Mullins described:
  - a deliberately "unfaithful retro" art style that sells a haunted cartridge
  - **ARG puzzle difficulty that has to scale with the size of the crowd solving it**
  - reaching out personally to over-zealous ARG players ([Game Developer recap](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/-i-inscryption-s-i-journey-from-game-jam-joint-to-cult-classic))

  A critique warns that a signature twist becomes predictable once your audience arrives *expecting* a hidden layer ([Puzzlebyrinth](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/en/articles/designer-mullins)).
- **FNAF [bg].** Hidden minigames and lore crumbs turned YouTube theorists into unpaid marketing, ending in a feature film that grossed about $290M.
- **The balance rule seen across these hits:** *answer the emotional questions completely* (what happened to this family, this friendship). *Leave the cosmological questions open* (who Gaster is, what the Pale King's world was).
  - Players forgive open lore. They do not forgive an unresolved emotional arc.

## 5. Music: how soundtracks go viral

- **Megalovania [bg].** It predates Undertale: it appeared in a 2008 EarthBound ROM hack and in Homestuck before becoming Sans's theme. It later reached Super Smash Bros. Ultimate (2019) and became among the most covered and remixed game tracks of its era. One composer also wrote the game, so the leitmotifs are dense:
  - "Once Upon a Time" returns in "Undertale" and "Hopes and Dreams."
  - "Fallen Down" stands for home and Toriel.
  - Flowey's jingle recurs as a threat.

  A small set of motifs repeated at emotional peaks trains players to cry *at the melody*. Fox's later Pokémon Sword/Shield credit shows how far that reach goes, including into Japan [bg].
- **OMORI [bg].** Music by Pedro Silva, Jami Lynne and bo en. The ending licensed bo en's existing song "My Time," which then had a second life on TikTok. The music is **diegetic** (a violin and piano recital is central to the plot), so the soundtrack *is* the story.
- **Celeste [bg].** Lena Raine's score, including "Resurrections," became a streaming staple and earned a Game Awards nomination for Best Score. Raine went on to compose for Minecraft.
- **Hollow Knight and Silksong [bg].** Christopher Larkin's character themes (Hornet's especially) bridge both games and give fans continuity across a seven-year gap.
- **Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (2025) [bg].** Not pixel art, but the clearest recent case of a soundtrack charting on its own. Lorien Testard's score drove huge streaming numbers and Game Awards wins.
- **To the Moon.** Gao composed cues for exact scene timings ([Super Jump](https://medium.com/super-jump/freebird-games-more-than-storytelling-69eece87a538)).
- **Fan music as a growth channel [bg].** The Living Tombstone's FNAF songs reached hundreds of millions of views. Fan songs need (a) a melodic motif simple enough to remix and (b) characters with strong first-person voices to write lyrics for.
- **Ways composers have been brought in:**
  - a solo auteur who is also the composer (Fox, Gao)
  - a small composer collective plus a licensed indie song (OMORI)
  - a dedicated composer embedded in the core team from day one (Larkin, Raine)

  Releasing the official soundtrack on Bandcamp and streaming services on launch day is now standard [bg].

## 6. Opening hooks (10, 30 and 120 minutes)

The constraint: Steam refunds any purchase within 14 days if it has been played for **under 2 hours** ([Steam Refunds](https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds/)). The game's thesis has to land before minute 120.

- **First 10 minutes: premise plus a jolt.**
  - Slay the Princess states its premise in its first line: a cabin, a princess in the basement, "you're here to slay her" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slay_the_Princess)).
  - Undertale's Flowey betrays you within minutes [bg].
  - Hades kills you and then *talks about it* [bg].
- **First 30 minutes:** the first character players love, plus the first clip-able moment, either a joke or a scare.
- **By 60 to 120 minutes:** the "this game is not what you think" turn. Examples [bg]:
  - Undertale's Toriel fight comes at roughly 45 to 60 minutes.
  - DDLC's Act 1 ending comes at roughly 1.5 to 2 hours.
  - Inscryption's first glimpse of the world outside the cabin comes in the first session.
- **Demos as a funnel.**
  - Deltarune's free Chapters 1 and 2 worked as a multi-year demo. The paid Chapters 3 and 4 then beat the demo's peak player count despite costing $25 ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/deltarune-chapters-3-and-4-are-finally-here-with-a-full-release-thats-blowing-up-steam-right-now)).
  - Slay the Princess's virality is covered in [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/slay-the-princess-how-did-this-horror) (further reading).
- **What keeps streamers playing [bg analysis]:**
  - a premise readable from a thumbnail
  - a reaction beat every 3 to 5 minutes (a joke, a choice or a scare)
  - low input load, so the streamer can talk while playing
  - choices chat can vote on
  - a twist that "chat must not spoil"
  - content warnings that double as a hook (as with DDLC and OMORI)

## 7. Humor

- Fox: "What's compelling about EarthBound is that it is not only funny and strange, but also sincere and emotional." The jokes land *because* the sincerity under them is real ([Killscreen](https://www.killscreen.com/behind-humor-toby-foxs-undertale/)). On satire versus instinct, he says: "I just write what's funny" ([The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/interview-undertale-game-creator-toby-fox/)).
- What comedy does for virality [bg analysis]:
  1. It lowers the player's guard so the rupture hits harder.
  2. It produces quotable lines and reaction clips.
  3. It produces fan art (comedic poses, outfit gags).
  4. It makes characters feel like friends, and people talk about their friends.
- Examples: Papyrus's puzzles, Spamton's sales-pitch delirium, Hades' banter, Pizza Tower's cartoon mania, and In Stars and Time's jokes about its own time loop [bg].
- **Caveat on puns:** wordplay and font jokes (Sans and Papyrus are named after typefaces) need transcreation. Undertale's 2017 Japanese version from 8-4 rewrote jokes rather than translating them [bg]. Visual and situational comedy travels best.

## 8. Worn-out tropes and how recent hits subverted them

| Worn trope | Why it's tired | Subversion that worked |
|---|---|---|
| "It was all a dream / coma / inner world" | Players now assume the colorful world is a mind-space. | OMORI tied the mind-space to *real-world* consequences and a confrontation among living friends. The dream is the *means*, not the twist [bg]. |
| Cute world with a dark secret, telegraphed | After DDLC, "cute = trap" is what players expect. | Put the darkness *on the box*. Slay the Princess, Look Outside (2025) and Mouthwashing (2024) announce dread up front, so the surprise is *how*, not *that* [bg]. |
| Save deletion and fourth-wall breaks | Peaked with Undertale and DDLC, and it reads as gimmick now. | In Stars and Time makes looping and saving the protagonist's *trauma*, so the meta-mechanic causes heartbreak rather than scares. Inscryption escalated into a real ARG [bg]. |
| Depression as a monster you beat | Reductive, and fights read as "kill your feelings." | Celeste: reconciliation with Badeline *becomes a mechanic* [bg]. |
| "You were the killer all along" | Expected since Spec Ops and OMORI. | Mouthwashing makes guilt clear early and uses a nonlinear timeline to make the question *how* and *why* [bg]. |
| Binary pacifist/genocide morality | Imitators copy Undertale's two-path structure. | Deltarune says "choices don't matter" and then hides a route. Slay the Princess turns choices into personality-mapped forms ([Comics Beat](https://www.comicsbeat.com/exclusive-slay-the-princess-the-pristine-cut-black-tabby-games/)). |
| Shock twist with no setup | Twists unearned by foreshadowing provoke backlash (12 Minutes, 2021) [bg]. | Every reveal should be re-readable: the second playthrough should show the clues were always there. |
| Signature "hidden layer" twist | The audience arrives looking for it ([Puzzlebyrinth](https://puzzlebyrinth.com/en/articles/designer-mullins)). | Make the hidden layer *emotional* rather than structural, or reveal the structure early and play with it openly. |

## 9. Cultural sensitivity and international reach

- **Evidence that these games travel:**
  - Deltarune drew same-day Japanese coverage ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2025/06/05/153453.html), [Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/news/260625t)).
  - Deltarune topped the Switch 2 eShop ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/detlarune/nintendo-switch-2-eshop-sales)).
  - OMORI has a Pixiv encyclopedia entry with an active edit history ([pixiv dic](https://dic.pixiv.net/history/OMORI/4557166)).
  - Japanese RPG Maker horror (Yume Nikki, Ib) is a direct ancestor of Western pixel story games, and Needy Streamer Overload shows Japan's "cute plus dark" (yami-kawaii) aesthetic selling worldwide [bg].
- **Themes that travel well:** friendship and found family, childhood nostalgia, grief, mercy and forgiveness, reconciling with a parent (Hades), and small-town belonging (Stardew).
- **Themes that travel poorly:**
  - country-specific political satire
  - religious iconography
  - jokes built on wordplay
  - US-centric school and cultural references that go unexplained
- **Handle with care:**
  - **Suicide and self-harm.** DDLC and OMORI show these can succeed, but only with clear content warnings and careful framing [bg].
  - **China.** Steam is global and not license-gated. A *licensed* Chinese release (via ISBN approval) faces restrictions on depictions of blood, skeletons and superstition [bg]. That matters for a skeleton-heavy cast.
  - **Gambling-like mechanics** are scrutinized in several markets, including Brazil and Belgium [bg].
- **Languages that pay:**
  - Simplified Chinese has ranked #1 or #2 among Steam languages in recent years ([Steam Hardware & Software Survey](https://store.steampowered.com/hwsurvey)) [bg on rank].
  - Also high value: Japanese, Brazilian Portuguese, Latin-American Spanish, Korean and Russian [bg].
  - Each region also has its own fan platform: Bilibili (China), Pixiv and X (Japan), and TikTok, Tumblr and AO3 (West) [bg].
  - Localize at launch, not later, so fan content in each language starts on day one.

## 10. Fifteen narrative and character principles, ranked

1. **Build a cast of distinct individuals, not mobs.** Every named NPC, including enemies, gets a silhouette, a color, a tic and a want. Each one is a chance at a fandom.
2. **Make every main character readable at low resolution:** one shape, one signature color, one prop that is easy to cosplay. Test by drawing it from memory in 60 seconds.
3. **Give characters a sincere core under the comedy.** The humor earns attention, and the sincerity converts it into devotion.
4. **Give each lead character a verbal tic shown through formatting** (case, brackets, punctuation, font) so lines become memes in plain text.
5. **Leave every lead with one deliberate gap** ("you never know what his deal is"). Ambiguity is where fan fiction and shipping live.
6. **Place a "game remembers you" moment across reloads or saves early on** (within the first 2 hours). Persistent memory is the most-discussed mechanic in the genre.
7. **Make outcomes comparable.** Endings, forms or routes should be nameable and shareable ("which one did you get?"). Consider showing global choice percentages.
8. **Land the thesis twist before minute 120** (Steam's refund window). Use a premise jolt in the first 10 minutes and a character to love by minute 30.
9. **Write the music around a few leitmotifs, and reprise them at peaks together with a player action.** That is the reliable "I cried at a pixel game" mechanism.
10. **Answer the emotional questions fully and leave the cosmological ones open.** Seed one Gaster-class unexplained figure and gate some evidence behind rare random events.
11. **Give the protagonist or cast a second version** (dream versus real, light versus dark). This gives fans alternate universes, double the merch and room for interpretation.
12. **Make the meta-mechanic emotional, not shocking.** Save, load, looping and the fourth wall should express a character's pain (as in ISAT), not just delete files.
13. **Put the darkness on the box.** Advertise the premise honestly and surprise players with *how* it unfolds. Telegraphed "cute world, dark secret" is now a cliché.
14. **Plan for an unspoilable reveal that turns fans into recruiters,** plus content warnings that double as a hook. Scale any ARG or cipher to the expected crowd size (Mullins).
15. **Localize Simplified Chinese, Japanese and Brazilian Portuguese at launch, and transcreate the humor.** Favor universal themes (friendship, grief, forgiveness, family) over culture-specific satire, and avoid content that blocks future licensed releases.
