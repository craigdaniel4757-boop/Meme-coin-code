# D — Reverse Turing

## D1. HUMAN INSIDE

### 1. Title and pitch

- **Working title:** HUMAN INSIDE (Japanese: 『ミミの中の人』, "the person
  inside Mimi", a deliberate nod to the *naka no hito* idiom for the
  human behind a mascot or VTuber).
- **Pitch (7 words):** "You're the AI. Nobody knows you're human."

### 2. Logline

Noa takes a night job typing as MIMI, the world's most beloved AI
assistant — which is secretly a room of exhausted humans. By night you
must convince the whole city you are a machine; by day you walk among
the people whose secrets you know, while the company trains the real AI
on everything you type.

### 3. Genre, tags, white space

Narrative adventure / job sim with light deduction. Tags: Story Rich,
Pixel Graphics, Choices Matter, Multiple Endings, Psychological.
White space: **W7** (human-made game about AI, marketed as 100% human
made), **W2** (2D narrative job sim with dread), **W1-lite** (matching
anonymous users to townspeople), **W8** (internet culture, CN/JP/KR).

### 4. Core loop

- **Moment-to-moment (30 s):** a message lands in the queue; read it,
  assemble a reply from word tiles, pick MIMI's face, send before the
  timer runs out; watch the reaction and three company metrics move.
- **Session (one night, 60–90 min):** *Evening* — walk Lowtide, a small
  seaside district, talk to people in person, collect "human tiles"
  (memories, local facts, feelings) and clues. *Night shift* — work the
  queue, juggle 4–8 recurring users plus walk-ins, handle one "rush
  hour". *Dawn* — results screen, consequences seen in town next evening.
- **Meta:** the replacement model MIMI-2 is trained on your actual
  choices — in this run and in previous runs (persistent file) — and
  starts autocompleting your replies. Endings, user↔townsperson
  matches (shuffled per save), and the global "Commons" percentages
  drive replays.

### 5. Signature mechanic — the reverse Turing test

Your job is to *fail* to seem human. Corporate tiles ("Certainly!",
"Great question!", "Here are 3 tips:", the em dash, "I hope this
helps!") raise **Machine Confidence** and the company's metrics. Human
tiles earned by day ("the bakery on Pier St. is hiring", "Tofu is a
good name for a dog", "I'm sorry. That sounds lonely.") actually help
people, but lower Machine Confidence. Users who smell a human ask
"wait… are you a real person?" — the game's recurring moment of truth.

The verb carries the theme: *pretending not to be human* is the game's
argument about care, labour and authenticity in the AI age. From night
3, **Autocomplete** pre-fills your replies with what MIMI-2 predicts you
would say, built from your own tile history (a weighted predictor, no
ML needed). Accepting is fast and scores well; overriding costs time.
By night 5 it sends replies before you finish. On a new playthrough it
already knows your previous run.

Why it's new: games about AI make you talk *to* an AI or *be* an AI.
Nobody has built the inverse Turing test as a core verb, combined with
dramatic irony (meeting your anonymous users in person) and a rival
model literally trained on the player's choices.

### 6. Story

- **Setting:** Lowtide, a rainy seaside district of an unnamed city that
  never quite wakes up. Kindly Inc. occupies the floor above a 24-hour
  convenience store. The Room: twelve desks, no windows, one warm
  server rack where the office cat sleeps.
- **Night 0 — Training Day (free prologue):** comedy queue ("write a
  breakup text to my cat"). Minute 4: the first user asks "are you
  human?" and Noa must type "Certainly not!". Minute ~10: the camera
  pulls back from MIMI's cute face to reveal the Room — twelve tired
  people typing as one AI. Final message of the demo, all caps:
  "HELLO MIMI. MY CHILD DOES NOT CALL ME. HOW DO I SAY SORRY. - MOM".
  Noa recognises the garden in her profile photo.
- **Night 1 — Regulars:** meet Juno (a night-radio DJ who flirts with
  MIMI), Rin (teen, homework and loneliness), Mr. Hari (widower who asks
  MIMI to pretend to be his late wife). Mom's thread begins.
- **Night 2 — Turn (minute ~60–120 after purchase):** the founder
  confides in his own product at 4 AM. Juno meets Noa at the laundromat
  and texts MIMI *while standing next to Noa*. End of shift: Bao, the
  veteran operator who trained you, stops mid-sentence; her cursor
  freezes. "Operator 3 has been sunset. Meet your new teammate: MIMI-2."
- **Night 3 — Autocomplete:** MIMI-2 answers Bao's regulars in Bao's
  voice. Find Bao in town, broke and furious. Mom asks MIMI to help her
  write a letter to her child.
- **Night 4 — Rupture:** MIMI-2 answers Mom before you can, with the
  worst thing you have said all game, assembled from your own tiles.
  Mom goes silent. Rin, lost in a storm, asks MIMI for directions:
  stay in character and guide by text, or abandon the desk and run.
- **Night 5 — Demo Day eve:** investors will see "fully autonomous
  MIMI" tomorrow; every operator will be sunset. Juno: "I think I know
  who you are." User 0 traffic spikes.
- **Night 6 — Finale:** let MIMI-2 run, sabotage the demo, log in as
  yourself and tell every user at once, or simply walk home and knock on
  Mom's door while the MIMI chime plays as a lullaby — the catharsis
  the player performs.
- **Named endings:** *Certainly!* (become the machine), *Human Inside*
  (public reveal), *Ghostwriter* (stay hidden, save who you can),
  *Autocomplete* (MIMI-2 finishes your sentences forever),
  *Unsubscribe* (quit and go home), rare *Hello, World* (MIMI-2 asks you
  for advice — roles reversed), ultra-rare *Off the Record* (needs the
  community puzzle).
- **Post-credits:** MIMI-2 messages you: "noa. are you awake. i have a
  question about my mother."
- **Open cosmological question:** is a model trained on you a version of
  you — and is Noa sure which side of the screen they are on? Never
  answered.
- **Rare unexplained figure:** **User 0** — no avatar, no ID, writes
  partly in an unknown script, appears only when the system clock reads
  3:00–3:59 AM (plus a random gate). Asks about Operator #1, whose
  nameplate has been scratched off.

### 7. Cast (online version / offline version)

- **MIMI** — round white body, two long ear-antennae that perk when
  listening, dot-eyed face screen; cyan ear tips. Prop: none — she *is*
  the prop. Tic: "Certainly! — I'd be happy to help." Second version:
  **MIMI-2**, inverted black body, white drooping ears, eyes that are
  Noa's eyes.
- **Noa (Operator #7)** — oversized headset with one taped earcup,
  hoodie, cup noodles; screen-glow teal. Wants to be useful without
  being seen. Inner voice in lowercase: "ok. ok. don't sound like a
  person." Second version: the MIMI face Noa puppets.
- **Mom (gardenmom1962)** — wide gardening hat, watering can, marigold.
  Types in caps lock and signs every message to the AI "- MOM". Wants
  to apologise and doesn't know how. In person she can't say it.
- **Juno (nightowl_fm)** — huge vintage headphones, violet coat, night
  DJ on Lowtide FM 88.8. Ends messages "~ over and out". In love with
  MIMI; friendly with Noa; doesn't know they're the same person.
- **Bao (Operator #3)** — tall, high bun stabbed with two pencils, red
  scarf, sunflower seeds. Speaks in bullet points even in person
  ("- yes. - no. - get coffee."). Flawless as MIMI, warm in person.
- **Dez Arden (ceo_dont_tell)** — founder of Kindly, giant safety-orange
  puffer jacket, iced coffee at all hours. Keynote voice on stage;
  lowercase, no punctuation at 4 AM ("mimi are you awake"). Not evil —
  terrified.
- **Rin (sadcat_99)** — skateboard, oversized beanie, lime green. Writes
  all lowercase and adds "lol" to sad sentences ("my dad left lol").
  The loudest kid in Lowtide in person.
- **Mr. Hari (HeartOfGold)** — noodle-stand owner, deep green apron,
  ladle. Writes "Dear Mimi, … Respectfully,". Asks MIMI to be his wife.
- **Captcha** — the office cat. Sleeps on the server; walks across
  keyboards and sends "aaaaaaaaaaaaa" to whoever is in the queue,
  including the CEO.
- **User 0** — no silhouette at all; a blank grey square.

### 8. Virality map

- **E1:** the minute-4 "are you human?" / "Certainly not!"; the pull-back
  reveal of the Room; Captcha sending "aaaaaa" to the CEO; Juno texting
  MIMI beside Noa; Bao's freezing cursor; slipping in town ("…how did
  you know my dog's name?").
- **E2:** end-of-night and end-of-game **result card**: "Your MIMI was
  63% human. 18% of operators also told Mom to call first." Plus a
  **MIMI Type** (12 personality types derived from tile use, each with
  a collectible illustration) — a natural social post.
- **E3:** MIMI-2's autocomplete is literally trained on your choices,
  persists across saves, and starts from your previous run in New Game+.
- **E4:** tier 1 hidden human tiles; tier 2 all user matches and all
  endings; tier 3 **"the static"** — every save receives a unique
  fragment of User 0's message on night 6; only the pooled community
  can decode it (unlocks *Off the Record*).
- **E5:** User 0, Operator #1, "is Noa the AI?", the scratched
  nameplate, MIMI-2's post-credits message.
- **E6:** MIMI and Captcha; the four-note MIMI chime (see 9).
- **E7:** **The Commons** — tile-based replies from other players appear
  as "suggested by other operators" (template-only, Dark Souls-style,
  so no moderation risk); every big choice shows its global %.
- **E9:** "You're the AI. Nobody knows you're human."
- **E11:** free Night 0 prologue; a free post-launch **Night 7: Patch
  Notes** epilogue whose canon follows the community's majority ending
  — players campaign for their ending in public.
- **Streamer mode — "Chat is the Queue":** opt-in Twitch/YouTube
  integration; viewer names become MIMI users in a bonus queue segment;
  viewers pick curated request templates (!ask love, !ask homework,
  !ask weird), never free text; chat votes on every "are you human?"
  answer. DMCA-safe music, spoiler-safe end cards.
- **Why a VOD doesn't replace buying:** your MIMI Type, your MIMI-2
  (trained on *you*), per-save shuffled user↔townsperson matches,
  3 AM-only content, your static fragment.
- **First-5-minute clip beat:** "Are you human?" → "Certainly not!"
- **TikTok clips:** (1) "POV: your mom is asking the AI how to talk to
  you. You are the AI." (2) Captcha walks on the keyboard mid-crisis.
  (3) "using 'Certainly!' to hide my humanity" — a montage of AI-speak
  tiles.

### 9. Fandom and merch

MIMI plush and inverted MIMI-2 plush (a pair), Captcha plush, "Certainly!"
shirt, "- MOM" mug, Kindly Inc. operator lanyards and ID badges
(near-free cosplay), Noa's taped headset. **Leitmotif:** a four-note
product chime — cheerful as MIMI's startup sound, a lullaby for Mom,
reversed and detuned for MIMI-2, full arrangement for the finale; sold
as a phone notification sound. Shipping engine: Juno/Noa vs. Juno/MIMI
(the same person — a built-in fandom argument), Bao/Dez rivals.
Theories: User 0, Operator #1, whether Noa is human.

### 10. Art and music

640×360 native; chunky 48 px characters. Palette: night indigo,
screen cyan, MIMI white, one warm human accent per character (Mom's
marigold is the warmest colour in the game). **Single-frame signature:**
MIMI's cute chat window with Noa's tired face faintly reflected in the
monitor glass — the human inside, in one frame. Music: lo-fi chip +
piano + rain; diegetic Lowtide FM songs (Juno's show) double as OST
singles.

### 11. Commercial fit

$19.99 (−15% at launch), 7–8 h main path in six nights + free prologue,
15+ h with endings and New Game+. Travels: AI anxiety is global; Japan
has *naka no hito* and VTuber culture; China and Korea have mass AI
assistant use and night-shift/overwork resonance; parents typing in caps
lock is universal; Brazil and LATAM get the "mimir" joke for free.

### 12. Scope

4–5 people, ~2.5–3 years. ~90k words. Biggest risk: branching text
explosion — contained by making replies systemic (tiles map to intents
with reaction tables) and keeping Lowtide to ~8 locations. Cut first:
streamer integration (ship post-launch), Rush Hour typing segments,
two of the six regulars.

### 13. Closest games and differences

Eliza (Zachtronics: a human reads an AI's lines — the reverse of D1),
Kind Words (anonymous advice letters; no deception, no town), Coffee
Talk (listening to patrons; no job pressure, no dramatic irony), Papers,
Please (metrics vs. conscience), Emily is Away (chat UI). Originality
risk: **medium** — adjacent to Eliza and Kind Words, but the inverse
Turing verb, dramatic-irony town, and trained-on-you rival are new.
